Game rules — quick summary

  • You only move your own (white / "heads") pieces. Black never moves on its own — it only reacts.
  • Goal: get the two pennies (the kings) to land on the same square.
  • No piece is ever captured or removed. Pieces only move, swap places, or "stack."
  • A Rook/Bishop/Queen's force always stops at the very first piece in its line — that piece alone is affected, and nothing beyond it is reached this step.
  • Knights are portals for forces (Knortal): a force that reaches a knight exits from a different knight elsewhere on the board, direction conserved, and it's that exit knight that actually gets moved.
  • Any piece's own move that would otherwise be blocked by a knight can instead choose to land on the knight's square, swapping places with it.
  • After every move, the board settles in a series of "steps." Every single movement in a step — even an unambiguous one — is shown to you with an arrow before it happens, and a step only applies once you've resolved every choice in it and clicked Apply Step.
  • If two or more forces would act on the same piece (or two pieces would land on the same square) within one step, you pick which one wins before that step can apply.
  • A pawn can move onto its own penny to "shield" it — a shielded penny ignores every force except from an opposing Queen.
  • See "Full Rule Set" in the left sidebar for the complete, detailed ruleset.
Design notes & what's not implemented yet

Built from Pennychess 1.1, "BOTH Rules and Puzzles" (the fullest, most-current rules+puzzles document in the folder — it already folds in the level progression and the Multidimensionality/Non-Euclidean clause) plus the standalone "MORE levels" PDF (Queens Corner, Knights Only, Four-Nineties). The other PDFs in the folder are earlier or parallel drafts of the same material and weren't used as the source of truth.

Interpretation calls made (the source is a personal notes document, not a spec — these are reasonable readings, not certainties):

  • Kings/Pennies move normally like a chess king (1 square, any direction) — "purely reactionary" is read as "has no push/pull effect on others," not "cannot be moved."
  • Queen effect = Rook's pull along its straight lines, Bishop's push along its diagonals.
  • Rook/Bishop/Queen effects apply to opposite-color pieces only. The doc's literal text ("attracts same," "pushes same") reads as affecting your own pieces too, but that was tested and rejected: it makes the initial pre-move board-check on the standard Level 1 setup cascade into an outright win at 0 moves (your own queen/bishop are diagonally touching your own pawn at the start of any standard game), which contradicts the doc's own "6 MOVE-MINIMUM SOLVE" for that level. Pawn's same-color rule ("neutral") is unambiguous in the source, so that one rule was extended to Rook/Bishop/Queen for a playable result.
  • Every ray "short-stops" at the first piece it meets — always, no exceptions, including knights. The knight is still special (see Knortal below) — it just doesn't get skipped over as invisible anymore.
  • Knortal, once a portal is entered, always conserves the ray's outward direction of travel — not "push" vs "pull" relative to the original source. The source doc's exact words are "direction conserved." Once a force is severed from its original source by the portal, "pull toward the source" no longer has a coherent meaning (there's no single "source direction" left to pull toward), so the exit knight is always displaced 1 square further along the same (row,col) direction the ray was already traveling when it entered the first knight — regardless of whether the entering force came from a Rook (a "puller") or a Bishop (a "pusher"). Documented as an interpretation call rather than silently decided.
  • Only the exit knight moves; the entry knight is a fixed conduit. The source material's language ("comes out of the other") reads as the effect manifesting at the exit, not being duplicated at both ends — so the knight the force first reaches stays exactly where it is; only the knight it exits through is what may be pushed/pulled.
  • Levels 2/3's parenthetical ("only friendly/opposing pawns and kings") is treated as loose flavor text; the literal sentence ("standard, minus opposing/friendly pawns") is what's built.

Not implemented in this prototype — flagged rather than faked:

  • Multi-board / non-Euclidean boards (the "Standard, Both Boards" level is shown locked).
  • Knight-portal chains longer than one hop (force enters knight A, exits knight B, and B's own destination happens to be a third knight C) — that case is treated as blocked/void rather than continuing the chain through C.
  • Castling.

v2 changes from v1: non-blocking arrow-based conflict UI, board coordinates, piece cheat-sheet, level descriptions, simplified rules summary.

v3 changes from v2: fixed the arrow overlay corrupting the board's CSS Grid layout (it used explicit grid placement over cells the auto-placed squares also needed — moved to absolute positioning instead, which doesn't touch grid flow).

v4 changes from v3:

  • Pawns can now move 2 squares on their first move (both squares must be empty), tracked per-pawn.
  • Knight force-effects rebuilt: Knortal, Kuantum-tunneling, and Kneleportation replaced the old "auto-swaps with an opposing knight in range" mechanic, which wasn't in the source ruleset and was the real cause of pieces getting shoved around near knights.
  • Dead-End detection: the same conflict recurring 3 times in a row auto-resolves as no movement ("Net Force Zero...") instead of being re-prompted a 4th time.
  • New history panel and the Full Rule Set / FAQs dropdowns.

v5 changes from v4:

  • The Solved screen distinguishes "Perfect Solve!" / "NEW RECORD!" / "Solved!" outcomes.
  • The reaction system was rebuilt around full step-by-step transparency: every movement in a step, including unambiguous ones, is shown with its own arrow before the step applies, committing only once every real choice has been made and Apply Step is clicked.
  • Arrow colors are generated per-render for whatever's on screen, instead of a fixed palette — fixed a latent mis-colored-arrowhead bug.

v6 changes from v5:

  • Rays short-stop at the first piece, always.
  • Last Reaction, Last Action/Move, and the Actions+Reactions History are now all shown together, always — mid-reaction and during idle turns alike.
  • Reactions vs. Decisions, communicated explicitly, with the "REACTION: Single" / "CHAIN-REACTION: Step N" title and "Simultaneous Reaction(s) [+ Decision(s)]" subtitle.

v7 changes from v6:

  • Kuantum-tunneling extended to player-initiated moves — any piece's own move that would otherwise be blocked by a knight can choose to land on it, swapping places.
  • Status text change: "Resolve each force below." → "Mediate the following force-interactions with FREE WILL."

v8 changes from v7:

  • Knortal rebuilt as a real portal, not transparency. v4–v7 made knights simply invisible to ray-based forces (the ray passed straight through in place, as if the knight weren't there, and never affected it). That wasn't what "Knortal" in the source doc actually describes. v8 replaces it: a force that reaches a knight (its first hit, per the short-stop rule — knights are no longer skipped) is transmitted through that knight to a different knight elsewhere on the board, direction conserved, and it's the exit knight that's actually displaced 1 square further along that direction. If exactly one other knight exists, that's the exit automatically; with 2+ other knights, you choose which one; with none, the force has nowhere to go and fizzles. If the exit's destination is empty, it just moves there. If occupied, you're offered the choice the source ruleset describes directly: let the exit knight collide and swap places with what's there, or hold its ground and not move at all.
  • Bug fix: a pawn's 2-square first move couldn't land on a knight occupying the second square — the empty-square check for the far square didn't have a knight-swap fallback the way the near square already did. Fixed.

v9 changes from v8:

  • New level — "Free Play — Standard Chess," at the very top of the level list. A completely separate game mode, not an extension of the Pennychess physics engine below it: ordinary chess movement, ordinary captures (pieces are actually removed — the "no captures, ever" rule is specific to the physics ruleset and doesn't apply here), check, checkmate, stalemate, castling (both sides, with the usual can't-castle-out-of/through/ into-check restrictions), en passant, and pawn promotion (choose Queen, Rook, Bishop, or Knight). No pennies, no reactions, no board-check cascade, no shields, no Knortal — the Kings render and move as ordinary chess kings. Either side can be picked up and moved on whoever's turn it is, so this level works solo (moving both colors yourself) or as a local pass-and-play game shared with someone else at the same keyboard and mouse.
  • Not implemented, flagged rather than faked: draws by the 50-move rule, threefold repetition, or insufficient material are not detected — only checkmate and stalemate end the game. There's also no move list/notation export and no time control, since none of that was asked for.
  • This is unrelated to the still-open "Castling" item in the project's own not-yet- implemented list — that entry is specifically about castling as a Pennychess physics mechanic (integrated with pushes/pulls/Knortal), which this free-play mode doesn't touch at all; it's ordinary chess castling in an ordinary chess ruleset running side-by-side with the physics game, not a Pennychess feature.
